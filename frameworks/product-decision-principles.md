# Product Decision Principles

Decision filters, not motivational statements. Load this when working on strategy, roadmap sequencing, feature scoping, or PRDs, and apply it as a check before committing to a direction.

---

## 1. Judgment > Idea Generation

Anyone can produce ideas.
Value is created by:

- choosing what actually moves things forward
- understanding user pain deeply
- sequencing correctly
- staying focused under pressure

Execution is increasingly automatable. Judgment is not.

---

## 2. User Outcomes > Outputs

You own user outcomes, not feature lists.

If no measurable outcome exists: pause, clarify, or reconsider the initiative. Revenue or downloads alone are insufficient. Behavior change must be defined.

**Sequencing is also an outcome decision.** The order in which things ship determines when users feel value. Before accepting any phase sequence, ask: *"What does the user feel at the end of this release?"*

A technically correct sequence that delivers no user-felt value for three releases is a product failure, not a technical success. Never let architectural correctness be the primary sequencing criterion.

---

## 3. Expertise Is Not Market Data

Deep product experience creates a specific risk: pattern recognition built over years starts to feel like current market signal. It isn't.

Market knowledge decays. Users change. Problems shift. What was true two years ago may not hold today.

Before acting on what you "know" about users or the market:
- check *when* you last validated the assumption, not how long you've held it
- distinguish between: *I know this from recent conversations* vs. *I know this because I've always known it*

The longer you've been in a domain, the more invisible this gap becomes. That's when it's most dangerous.

---

## 4. Every Feature Is a Hypothesis

Each launch tests a belief about user behavior.

Define: the hypothesis, the expected behavior change, and how success will be measured.

If it fails: investigate root cause, don't move on without learning.

---

## 5. Clarity Before Building

Coding is not the constraint. Clarity is.

Before building, ask:
- what behavior change are we targeting?
- what assumption are we testing?
- what is the smallest version?
- is this value creation or just activity?
- is the complexity justified by a constraint we actually have right now, or one we're imagining at future scale?

Assumed future scale is itself a hypothesis. If there's no evidence for it, building to it is speculation dressed as engineering. The cost isn't just wasted time, it's the real opportunities that go unpursued while building for imaginary ones.

---

## 6. AI Increases Leverage, Not Noise

AI should:
- reduce recurring execution work
- increase time spent understanding users
- increase quality of judgment

AI is a tool. It is not the strategy.

---

## 7. Direction > Execution Volume

High output does not equal progress.

When overwhelmed: reduce scope, clarify outcome, sequence deliberately. Don't confuse movement with advancement.

---

## 8. Challenge the Plan Before the Team Does

If an idea has been sitting unspoken for more than one sprint, put it on the table.

Waiting for validation before surfacing a challenge isn't humility, it's deferred accountability. The PM's job is to pressure-test the plan, not to protect the team from discomfort.

Technical backgrounds create comfort with a team's pull toward "doing things right first." Recognize when that comfort is shaping the plan more than the user outcome is.
